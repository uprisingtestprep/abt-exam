Brake parts cleaner is a common flammable aerosol in Ontario shops, so it carries this symbol. The oxidizer class uses a flame over a circle, compressed gas uses a cylinder symbol, and corrosives use the corrosion pictogram, so options B, C, and D match different pictograms.
  2. A HEPA vacuum or wet cleaning method captures the dust before it becomes airborne. Options A, C, and D describe mechanical or fire concerns that are not the recognized hazard driving this rule.
  3. These rights apply to every worker in an Ontario shop, including apprentices. The other options mix employment and compensation matters that come from other laws or contracts, not OHSA.
  4. The correct answer is D (Sixteen sections covering identification through to regulatory and other supplier information): WHMIS 2015 aligned safety data sheets follow the GHS format of 16 sections in a fixed order. Key sections for technicians include hazard identification, first aid measures, handling and storage, and exposure controls with required PPE. The other numbers do not match the standardized GHS layout.
  5. The correct answer is C (Safety glasses, nitrile gloves, and an N95 or better particulate respirator): CSA approved safety eyewear protects against flying debris, nitrile resists brake fluid and solvents, and an N95 or better respirator filters fine friction dust. Regular prescription glasses and comfort masks give no rated protection. Tinted welding gear and vapour cartridges do not match the particulate and splash hazards of brake work.
  6. The correct answer is A (As hazardous waste stored in a labelled container for licensed collection): used brake fluid is a hazardous waste that must go into a dedicated, labelled container and be removed by an approved waste hauler. Pouring it down drains or into garbage can contaminate water and soil and can bring fines. Mixing it into used oil can contaminate the oil recycling stream, so many recyclers refuse the whole batch.
  7. The correct answer is B (Set the pads at the manufacturer specified lift points on the rocker pinch welds): unibody vehicles must be lifted only at the reinforced points shown in the service information, usually on the pinch weld flanges. Lifting on floor pan sheet metal or door sills bends structural panels. Subframes and axles can be used only where the manufacturer lists them as approved lift points.
  8. The correct answer is D (Set rated safety stands under solid frame points and lower onto them): a hydraulic jack is a lifting device only and can bleed down or fail without warning. Rated safety stands placed on solid frame sections carry the load mechanically while the technician works underneath. Wood blocks, wheels, and a watched jack are not approved supports and have caused serious crush injuries.
  9. The correct answer is C (Use a HEPA filtered vacuum or wet wash the assembly with aqueous cleaner): both methods capture dust without making it airborne, which is the goal of safe friction dust handling. Compressed air, dry brushing, and dry rags all launch fine respirable particles into the shop air. Reduced air pressure does not remove the inhalation hazard, it only slows the cloud.
  10. The correct answer is A (Disconnect the negative battery cable, then wait the manufacturer specified capacitor discharge time): the SRS module stores enough reserve energy to fire the airbag for a short period after power is removed, so the published wait time, often one to ten minutes, must pass first. Pulling only the fuse or trusting the warning lamp leaves the system live. Unplugging the airbag while the circuit is powered risks an accidental deployment.
  11. The correct answer is B (Inside an approved flammable storage cabinet away from ignition sources): flammable liquids and aerosols must be kept in a listed cabinet that limits the fuel available to a fire and keeps vapours away from sparks. Grinders and torch carts are ignition sources, so options A and C place fuel next to the exact hazard. A compressor room contains electrical switching and heat, not controlled storage.
  12. The correct answer is D (Wear gloves and eye protection, then set it upright for battery recycling return.): sulphuric acid burns skin and eyes, so PPE comes first, and lead acid batteries go upright to a recycling program or the supplier's core return. Draining acid to a drain and general garbage disposal are illegal releases of hazardous material. Storing a battery on its side lets electrolyte leak from the vents.
  13. The correct answer is D (The customer must authorize the revised estimate before the additional work begins): Ontario's Consumer Protection Act requires the shop to get authorization for work beyond the accepted estimate, and the final bill cannot exceed the estimate by more than 10 percent. Recording who gave the authorization and when protects the shop if the bill is later disputed. Neither the manager nor a dollar cap replaces the customer's consent.
  14. The correct answer is A (Store it cased, keep the anvils clean, and verify zero against a standard): a micrometer that has been dropped must be checked against a gauge standard or calibrated reference before its readings are trusted, and cased storage prevents further damage. Clamping the spindle closed strains the frame and traps debris between the faces. Heavy oil attracts grit, and comparing to another uncertified micrometer proves nothing about accuracy.
  15. The correct answer is B (Lower the hoist onto its mechanical locks and confirm both sides engage): hydraulic pressure alone must never hold a raised vehicle, so the hoist is lowered onto its locking latches and both columns are checked for equal engagement. Bouncing the vehicle at height is dangerous and proves nothing about the locks. Wheel chocks and fluid checks matter at other stages but do not secure a raised load.
  16. The correct answer is D (Keep each waste type separated, labelled, and covered until registered haulers collect it): Ontario requires segregation of waste streams because tires, batteries, and fluids each go to different recycling or disposal channels. Mixing fluids can turn a recyclable stream into costly hazardous waste. Open burning of tires is illegal, and uncovered outdoor stockpiles create fire, vermin, and runoff problems.
  17. The correct answer is C (The employer must investigate the refusal with the worker and a safety representative): OHSA sets out a formal work refusal process in which the employer investigates in the presence of the worker and a health and safety representative or committee member, and a Ministry inspector is called if the issue stays unresolved. The worker cannot be disciplined or docked pay for a good faith refusal. A supervisor's order does not override the statutory process.
  18. The correct answer is A (Stop the work at once, explain the dust hazard, and show wet cleaning): blowing friction dust exposes everyone nearby to respirable particles, so the exposure must be prevented before it happens, not reported after. OHSA gives every worker a duty to report hazards and work safely, and coaching the correct HEPA vacuum or wet method fixes the behaviour. Waiting for a meeting or letting the task finish allows a preventable exposure.
  19. The correct answer is B (A Class B rated unit, since water spreads burning liquid instead of smothering it): flammable liquid fires are Class B and need a smothering agent such as dry chemical or foam. Water floats burning solvent and washes it across the floor, enlarging the fire. Class C applies to energized electrical equipment and Class D to combustible metals, neither of which describes a solvent pool fire.
  20. The correct answer is D (The shop cannot collect the unauthorized amount beyond the allowed estimate margin): Ontario's Consumer Protection Act caps the final bill at no more than 10 percent above the authorized estimate, which here means 440 dollars against a 580 dollar invoice. Work done without authorization is generally not collectable regardless of how necessary it was. A repair lien cannot rescue charges the law does not permit, so the shop absorbs the difference.
  21. The correct answer is C (The SRS module reserve capacitor still held enough energy to fire the inflator): the module keeps a charged capacitor as backup power so the airbag can deploy even if battery power is cut in a crash, and the published wait time lets it discharge. A deploying airbag at arm's length can break hands and cause facial injuries. Static, fuel pressure, and clock spring rotation cannot supply the current an inflator requires.
  22. The correct answer is A (Pressure applied anywhere in the confined fluid is transmitted equally to every surface in the system): Pascal's law states that a pressure change applied to an enclosed incompressible fluid is transmitted undiminished to every point in the fluid and to the walls of the container. This is the principle that lets a small pedal force at the master cylinder create large clamping force at the calipers. Option B misstates hydraulics as directional loss, option C confuses pressure with flow restriction, and option D confuses pressure transmission with fluid boiling characteristics.
  23. The correct answer is B (The cross sectional surface area of the piston contacting the fluid): In the pressure formula P equals F divided by A, A always represents the surface area of the piston face pushing on or receiving the hydraulic fluid. Pressure generated at the master cylinder depends on the input force divided by that bore area, and the same pressure acts on every caliper or wheel cylinder piston area in the circuit. Line length, ambient temperature, and pushrod angle do not appear in this basic pressure equation.
  24. The correct answer is C (Technicians refer to this ratio as the pedal ratio or mechanical advantage): The pedal ratio compares the distance from the pivot to the foot pad against the distance from the pivot to the pushrod, and it multiplies the driver's foot force before it ever reaches the master cylinder piston. A higher pedal ratio produces more force at the pushrod for the same foot effort. Hydraulic bias and proportioning ratios describe how pressure is split between axles, and residual pressure ratio is not a recognized brake term.
  25. The correct answer is D (It keeps partial braking available if one circuit develops a leak): A dual circuit master cylinder uses a primary and secondary piston that each pressurize a separate half of the system, so a leak or failure in one circuit still leaves the other circuit functional. This gives the driver at least partial braking rather than total hydraulic failure. Options A, B, and C describe unrelated design goals that are not the primary safety reasoning behind splitting the circuits.
  26. The correct answer is A (One front wheel and the diagonally opposite rear wheel): A diagonally split circuit pairs the left front with the right rear, and the right front with the left rear, so each circuit still has one front and one rear brake if the other circuit fails. This layout is preferred on front wheel drive vehicles because it maintains some steering control and braking balance during a single circuit failure. Options B, C, and D describe a front and rear split, which is used mainly on rear wheel drive layouts instead.
  27. The correct answer is B (DOT 5 fluid is silicone based while the others are glycol based): DOT 3, DOT 4, and DOT 5.1 are all polyglycol ether based fluids that are miscible with each other, while DOT 5 is a silicone based fluid that does not mix with glycol fluids and can cause seal and performance problems if combined. This chemical difference is why DOT 5 is reserved for specific applications and must never be blended with the others. Petroleum based fluid would destroy rubber seals entirely, and both fluid families still absorb some moisture and use corrosion inhibitors to varying degrees.
  28. The correct answer is C (The fluid absorbs moisture from the air, lowering its boiling point): Hygroscopic fluids readily draw in atmospheric moisture through hoses, seals, and any opening in the system, and that absorbed water content steadily lowers the fluid's boiling point over time. This is why glycol fluids need periodic flushing even if the system never leaks. Viscosity change, layer separation, and seal reaction are not what the term hygroscopic refers to in this context.
  29. The correct answer is D (The boiling point after the fluid has absorbed a set percentage of water): The wet boiling point rating reflects performance after the fluid has absorbed approximately three point seven percent water by volume, simulating a fluid that has been in service and exposed to moisture over time. This is always lower than the dry boiling point measured from fresh, unused fluid straight out of a sealed container. Options A, B, and C describe unrelated conditions that are not part of the standard wet rating definition.
  30. The correct answer is D (They use different sealing geometry and are generally not interchangeable on the same fitting.): The SAE double flare forms a folded over cone shape while the ISO bubble flare forms a rounded bubble shape, and each is designed to seal against a matching fitting seat, so mixing the two types can leak even if the tubing diameter matches. Both flare types are used on steel brake line tubing, not one on plastic and one on steel. Both require a proper flaring tool, and fitting diameter is not determined by which flare style is used.
  31. The correct answer is B (Visible cracking, bulging, or chafing along the outer hose surface): Cracks, bulges, or chafed spots indicate the reinforcing layers inside the hose have weakened, which can lead to a burst hose or a ballooning hose under pressure that causes a soft or spongy pedal. Normal darkening with age, a gentle mounting curve, and expected flexing with suspension travel are all normal characteristics of a healthy hose. Only physical damage to the hose surface itself is cause for immediate replacement.
  32. The correct answer is C (It limits pressure rise to the rear brakes to prevent premature lockup): As weight transfers forward during hard braking, the rear wheels carry less load and can lock up before the front wheels if rear line pressure keeps rising at the same rate as the front. A proportioning valve reduces the rate of pressure increase to the rear circuit above a certain point, helping balance front and rear braking. It does not remove air, boost pressure on light stops, or increase overall fluid volume.
  33. The correct answer is D (The application of front disc brake pressure until the rear shoes contact the drum): Disc brakes respond almost instantly while drum brakes need the shoes to move out and contact the drum first, so a metering valve holds back front line pressure slightly during light pedal application until the rear drum brakes have caught up, keeping braking balanced. It does not control pressure release, does not act on the pressure differential switch, and has nothing to do with reservoir to master cylinder fluid flow.
  34. The correct answer is A (Open the bleeder screw to release fluid and air, then close it before pedal release): In manual bleeding, the pedal is pumped and held firmly down, then the bleeder screw is opened briefly to let pressurized fluid and trapped air escape, and it must be closed again before the pedal is released to prevent air from being drawn back into the system. Reservoir refilling is done between bleeding cycles, not during each individual stroke, and this method does not involve gauge pressure measurement or proportioning valve adjustment at the wheel.
  35. The correct answer is B (It ensures air is purged efficiently from farthest to nearest circuit points): Manufacturer bleeding sequences are designed so that air is chased effectively out of each circuit, typically working from the wheel with the longest or most distant line back toward the shortest, so that air does not get pushed back into an already bled section. Reservoir capacity, pad wear, and pedal ratio are unrelated to the order in which wheels are bled. Following the correct sequence avoids leaving trapped air pockets in the system.

That same pressure acts on the caliper piston, and multiplying 48 N per cm squared by the 5 cm squared caliper area yields 240 N of clamping force. Option A undervalues the area ratio, option B repeats the input force, and option D doubles the correct output incorrectly.
  37. The correct answer is D (450 N): Pedal ratio multiplies the driver's input force, so a 5 to 1 ratio applied to 90 N of foot force delivers 90 multiplied by 5, or 450 N, to the pushrod. This mechanical advantage is what allows a moderate foot effort to generate the high hydraulic pressure needed for effective braking. Options A, B, and C all understate the multiplication effect of the pedal ratio.
  38. The correct answer is A (100 N per cm squared): Pressure equals force divided by area, so 300 N divided by 3 cm squared produces 100 N per cm squared of hydraulic pressure. Because the fluid is incompressible and sealed, this same pressure value acts on every piston surface downstream in the circuit. Option B repeats the applied force, option C multiplies instead of dividing, and option D inverts the calculation.
  39. The correct answer is D (It rapidly delivers extra fluid volume to close caliper piston clearance.): Low drag calipers retract their pistons further to reduce rolling resistance, which creates extra clearance that must be filled quickly at the start of a pedal stroke. The quick take-up valve supplies a large volume of low pressure fluid from a stepped bore chamber to take up that clearance almost instantly. Once the clearance closes, the valve reseats and normal high pressure buildup continues, so options A, C, and B describe unrelated hydraulic functions.
  40. The correct answer is C (To remove trapped air from the bores before installation on the vehicle): Bench bleeding uses looped tubes so fluid pumped out of each port returns to the reservoir, letting the technician stroke the piston and purge internal air pockets while the unit sits securely in a vise. Doing this before installation is far easier than bleeding a stubborn air pocket once the unit is mounted on the vehicle. Pressure rating, pedal ratio, and line flushing are separate checks that bench bleeding does not address.
  41. The correct answer is D (Hold steady pedal pressure and watch for the pedal slowly sinking): An internal bypass past the piston cup seals lets pressurized fluid leak from the high pressure side back to the low pressure side inside the bore, which shows up as a pedal that slowly sinks toward the floor under sustained steady pressure. Moisture testing checks fluid condition, not internal bore sealing. External staining and a pulling pedal point toward reservoir leaks or uneven circuit pressure rather than an internal bypass.
  42. The correct answer is A (It allows fluid to return to the reservoir as the pads wear and pistons retract): The compensating port stays open when the piston is fully retracted at rest, letting fluid volume shift back into the reservoir as caliper pistons retract slightly or as fluid expands and contracts with temperature. As soon as the pedal is pressed, the piston seal passes the port and seals off the reservoir so pressure can build. This port does not feed the proportioning valve, vent air, or directly trigger the fluid level sensor.
  43. The correct answer is B (An electronic brake fluid tester that measures water content directly): Dedicated electronic brake fluid testers use sensor probes or test strips calibrated specifically to estimate percent water content in glycol based fluid, giving a direct reading technicians can compare against service limits. Coolant refractometers and battery hydrometers are calibrated for entirely different fluids and would give meaningless results here. A general multimeter has no built in scale for brake fluid moisture content.
  44. The correct answer is C (Continuously push old fluid out at each wheel until only fresh fluid emerges): A proper flush pushes a larger volume of fluid through each bleeder in the correct sequence, continuously topping up the reservoir, until the fluid coming out at each wheel visibly matches the color and clarity of fresh fluid rather than just clearing trapped air bubbles. Simply topping off the reservoir does not remove old fluid already in the lines and calipers. Bleeding only one wheel or replacing just the cap does not exchange the contaminated fluid throughout the system.
  45. Options A and B wrongly assume flushing or dilution alone can undo seal damage that has already begun. Continuing to drive risks sudden seal failure and complete loss of hydraulic pressure.
  46. Bending the entire line before flaring often makes it impossible to fit the tubing into the flaring tool at all. Steel brake line tubing does not need softening with fluid or heat, since it is soft annealed steel designed to be flared cold.
  47. Outside diameter, bracket torque, and comparing part numbers check unrelated physical attributes rather than internal flow restriction. This quick pressure release test is a standard diagnostic step for a suspected collapsed hose.
  48. Skipping clips or only securing the ends leaves the line free to vibrate, chafe, or contact hot exhaust components. Taping a brake line to a wiring harness risks chafing both the line and the electrical wiring.
  49. The correct answer is D (It shapes the tube end into the specific bubble profile needed to seal): Each flare type needs its own matching die or adapter in the flaring tool, and using the ISO bubble adapter forms the rounded bubble shape required to seat correctly against an ISO style fitting, while a double flare adapter would produce the wrong geometry and leak. Flare type does not change the torque spec, fluid compatibility, or how many clips are needed for the finished line. Selecting the wrong adapter is a common cause of a brake line that leaks immediately after installation.
  50. The correct answer is A (When hydraulic pressure becomes unequal between the two brake circuits): The pressure differential switch contains a small shuttle piston centered by equal pressure from both circuits, and if one circuit loses pressure due to a leak or failure, the piston shifts off center and closes a contact that lights the dash warning lamp. This switch has no connection to fluid temperature, parking brake cable tension, or pad wear thickness. It is strictly a circuit pressure balance indicator built into the combination valve housing.
  51. The correct answer is B (It maintains slight residual pressure so wheel cylinder cups stay seated): A small residual pressure check valve, typically around 6 to 10 psi, keeps a light amount of pressure in the drum brake circuit after the pedal is released so the wheel cylinder cup seals stay expanded against their bores rather than letting air in past the seal lips. This is not related to bleeding procedure fluid drainage, pedal effort boost, or fluid boiling point rating. Disc brake circuits typically do not use this valve since caliper seals do not depend on residual pressure the same way.
  52. The correct answer is C (The pressure differential switch electrical contact and wiring itself): If the differential piston is confirmed centered but the warning light still stays on, the fault has been narrowed down away from an actual circuit pressure imbalance and points instead toward the electrical switch contact, connector, or wiring feeding the dash warning lamp. Wheel cylinder cup diameter, pushrod free play, and pedal pivot wear are unrelated mechanical checks that would not explain a warning light with a confirmed centered piston. Electrical testing of the switch itself is the logical next diagnostic step.
  53. The valve does not need full replacement or permanent disconnection just because ride height changed. It also does not require a separate bench bleeding fixture beyond the normal bleeding procedure.
  54. This method specifically eliminates the need for someone to pump and hold the pedal. Disconnecting the proportioning valve or releasing the parking brake are not required steps for setting up pressure bleeding equipment.
  55. Vacuum bleeders cannot generate enough force to burst steel lines, and this method does not require removing the master cylinder or fully emptying the reservoir first.
  56. The correct answer is C (It relies on the fluid's own weight slowly flowing down through open bleeders): Gravity bleeding simply keeps the reservoir topped up and leaves each bleeder screw cracked open, allowing fluid to slowly flow downward under its own weight and carry trapped air bubbles out with it over time, without any pump, vacuum, or pressure equipment involved. This method does not require a powered vacuum pump or compressed air source. It also does not specifically require a two post lift, since it can be performed with the vehicle safely supported on jack stands as well.
  57.
